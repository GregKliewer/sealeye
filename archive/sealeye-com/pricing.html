Pricing

One price per app. Everything included.

seaLeye is priced by the app under management, not by seats, tokens or runs. The models, the agents, the hosting and the support are all in the price, so your bill follows the apps your developers serve themselves, and nothing else.

What's included

Everything seaLeye runs is in the price.

There are no seats, no token meters and no charges per run. Using seaLeye more never costs more, so your developers never have a reason to go back to the queue.

  • The front door agent and the discovery agent, and every model call they make.
  • Ingesting your Platform Library, and ingesting it again whenever it changes.
  • Every environment, from the first dev environment to production.
  • Every run, change and promotion.

What isn't included is your cloud bill.

Environments run in your own accounts, so you pay your cloud provider directly, as you do today. seaLeye never resells or marks up cloud usage.

What counts as an app

An app is something your developers ship.

An app is one deployable unit with its own pipeline, together with every environment it runs in, from the first dev environment to production.

  • Environments don't add to the count. Dev, test, staging and production for one app are one app.
  • A repository that holds three services which deploy separately is three apps.
  • An app counts from the day its first environment goes live through seaLeye until the day it's decommissioned.

Size

Up to 250,000 lines of code, an app counts as one. Each further 250,000 lines, or part of that, counts as one more. So a 600,000-line app counts as three.

Lines are counted the way any standard line counter counts them:

  • Code only, without blank lines or comments.
  • Without tests, vendored dependencies, generated code, or the plan files seaLeye writes.

Tests never count, so nobody pays more for writing them.

Size is counted when an app is added and again at renewal. An app that grows during the year never changes the bill mid-term.

The annual commitment

  • You commit to a number of apps for the year, and pay for that number.
  • Add apps at any time at the same price, prorated to your renewal date.
  • Your commitment is a number, not a list. When you decommission an app, a new one can take its place at no charge.
  • The price per app is fixed for the term of your agreement.
  • At renewal, we count again.
  • Above 500 apps, ask us about volume pricing.

Sandboxed apps: room to experiment, walled off from production.

Sometimes the best use of your platform is one nobody has thought of yet. A sandboxed app is for innovation work: a developer gets direct, but strictly limited, access to the cloud console to experiment and discover new uses of the platform. Sandboxed apps are free. They never count as apps under management, and they are never on the path to production.

The walls

  • Its own account. Every sandboxed app gets a cloud account of its own, apart from every account on the path to production. It's opened through your platform team's own account automation.
  • Strictly limited console access. The developer signs in to that one account with short-lived credentials. Only the services and regions your platform team allows for sandboxes are available.
  • No production data. No customer data, no production data, and no network path to production or to your internal networks.
  • A spending cap. Your platform team sets it. When a sandbox reaches it, seaLeye shuts the sandbox down.
  • Thirty days. Then seaLeye tears the sandbox down. An extension needs your platform team's yes.
  • One at a time. Each developer can have one sandboxed app open at a time.
  • Never promoted. Nothing in a sandbox can be promoted, copied into a pipeline or turned into an environment.
  • Recorded. Who opened it, what it was for, what it cost, and when it closed.

How one starts

Like everything in seaLeye, a sandbox starts with a plan.

  1. The developer says what they want to try.
  2. They read the plan: the account, the limits, the cap and the end date.
  3. They approve it.

Inside the walls, they work in the console directly. That's the point of a sandbox, and it's why the walls are there.

What comes out

What comes out of a sandbox is what the developer learned. When an experiment shows something your platform should offer, seaLeye records it as a need, with a record of what was tried, and puts it in front of your platform team. If they build it, it arrives as an approved module, and every team can use it through the front door.

What it costs

Sandboxed apps cost nothing from seaLeye. The cloud resources inside them run in your own account, under the cap your platform team set.

FAQ

Pricing questions

Why price by the app?

Because the app is what your developers ship, and what your platform team used to provision by hand. The price follows that value, not our costs: you never pay for tokens, seats or runs.

Why count lines of code?

It's a measure you can check yourself with any standard line counter, and most apps never come near the cap. It counts code rather than infrastructure, so using more of your platform never costs more. The cap is there so that a very large codebase counts for what it is.

Who pays for the cloud?

You do, directly, as you do today. Environments run in your own accounts under your own billing, and seaLeye never marks up cloud usage.

Are model costs extra?

No. Every model call the agents make is in the price. If you run seaLeye in your own cloud account or on premises, you use your own approved model endpoint. The model is then yours to run, and the price per app is lower.

Is there a limit on runs?

Not for people. Every run, change and promotion a person starts is included. Automated runs, from a pipeline or a schedule, are covered up to a fair-use level agreed in your order form. We talk with you before anything changes.

What happens to the pilot fee?

If you go ahead, all $50,000 comes off your first year.

What do sandboxed apps cost?

Nothing from seaLeye, and they never count as apps under management. The cloud resources inside them run in your account, under your cap.

Can a sandboxed app become a production app?

No. A sandbox is never on the path to production. An idea gets to production by becoming a need, and then an approved module in your Platform Library. Once it's there, any team can use it through the front door.

What if we stop using seaLeye?

Nothing in your platform breaks, and there is nothing to buy back. Your environments keep running in your accounts. Your plans stay in your repositories as readable files. Your pipelines and modules keep working exactly as they do today.

What currency are the prices in?

US dollars, before taxes.
