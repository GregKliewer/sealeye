Security

Built to clear bank onboarding.

Every large financial institution puts a new SaaS vendor through InfoSec, Compliance, Audit, Third-Party Risk, Model Risk and Privacy reviews, and that process routinely takes longer than the pilot. seaLeye was built by people who ran platform teams inside banks, so the answers arrive before the questions do.

Principles

How seaLeye is built

seaLeye is a front door, not a new platform. Everything it does, it does through the automation and the controls you already run, and every principle below follows from that.

  • It reads your Platform Library read-only.
  • It acts through your pipelines and infrastructure as code, never around them.
  • It runs with the developer's own cloud credentials, so environments land in your accounts under your policies.
  • Nothing deploys until a person approves a plan they can read.
  • It never stores secrets. Only names are kept, with values redacted.
  • Customer code is never used to train models.

Deployment options

Run it where your risk appetite says to.

The product is the same in all four. What changes is who operates it and where it sits.

SaaS

Operated by seaLeye in a Canadian or US region, with your data kept in the region you choose.

Dedicated tenant

A single-tenant instance operated by seaLeye for your organization alone.

Your cloud account

Deployed into an account you own, inside your network boundary and under your policies.

On premises

Containers in your own data centre, operated by your own teams.

Answering Audit

Who asked for what, who approved it, and whether what is running now is still that.

What they'll ask

  • Can you show us every change and who approved it?
  • How do we know the evidence was not edited?
  • Will it fit our change management process?

What we hand them

  • Every plan, approval, deployment and change is recorded.
  • Evidence files are generated from the record, never typed in.

Answering Third-Party Risk

Whether seaLeye is a vendor you can depend on, and whether you can leave.

What they'll ask

  • Can you complete our standard questionnaires?
  • Who else handles our data?
  • What happens if you have an outage, or disappear?
  • How hard is it to leave?

What we hand them

  • Pre-completed SIG and CAIQ questionnaires
  • The sub-processor list
  • Business continuity and disaster recovery plans
  • A clean exit story: seaLeye drives your automation rather than replacing it, so if you ever remove it, your pipelines and modules keep running exactly as they do today.

Answering Model Risk

Where AI makes a decision, where it does not, and who is accountable.

What they'll ask

  • Where is AI used, and where is it not?
  • Can a model change our infrastructure on its own?
  • How do we inventory and validate it?

What we hand them

  • Where AI is used: the interview, planning, and library ingestion.
  • Where it isn't: execution runs through your own automation.
  • A person approves every plan.
  • Plans are readable files kept next to the code.
  • Model inventory documentation for OSFI Guideline E-23 and the Federal Reserve's SR 11-7.

Answering Privacy

FAQ

Security questions

Does seaLeye need administrator access to our cloud accounts?

No. It runs with the developer's own cloud credentials, so it can do only what that developer is already allowed to do, and every environment lands in your accounts under your policies.

What does the model see?

The code and configuration of the app being planned, the developer's answers, and your Platform Library. A plan names the settings an app reads and never their values, and anything seaLeye keeps holds only those names, with the values redacted.

Can we keep everything inside our own network?

Yes. Deployed in your own cloud account or on premises in containers, seaLeye runs inside your boundary and uses the model endpoint your organization has approved.

What happens to our platform if we stop using seaLeye?

Nothing. seaLeye drives your pipelines and infrastructure as code rather than replacing them, so they keep running exactly as they do today. The plans stay in your repositories as readable files.
