Use cases

Who feels the wait, and what changes when it ends.

Every use case below is the same product doing its one job: letting a team serve itself through the platform you already run. They are grouped by who feels the problem first.

Developers Platform engineering Business-line leaders Risk and audit

Developers

Ship without waiting, in the tools you already use.

The first dev environment, in minutes

The problem

A new app has code and nowhere to run it. The request goes into the platform team's queue, and the developer waits weeks for an environment they could describe in a sentence.

What seaLeye does

The developer runs sealeye run in the app's folder. The front door agent reads the code, asks what the folder cannot answer, and writes a plan from your approved modules. On approval, your pipeline builds it.

What changes

The first environment arrives in minutes, not weeks, and it is built exactly the way the platform team would have built it.

Changing infrastructure as the app changes

The problem

The app starts reading a new setting, or needs a place to keep files. Every change to what it runs on is another ticket, so developers work around the environment instead of changing it.

What seaLeye does

Run it again. seaLeye reads the app as it is now and shows the plan as a difference from what is deployed: unchanged, changed, new or removed. The change goes through the same modules and the same approvals.

What changes

Infrastructure keeps pace with the code, and every change has a plan a person approved.

Promoting a build to production, through your gates

The problem

Getting from dev to test to production means learning each stage's rules, filing each stage's request, and waiting on each stage's team.

What seaLeye does

The plan records what the app is designed for. seaLeye moves the build through your test stages to production through the pipeline gates you already enforce, and never around them.

What changes

Promotion becomes a step the developer takes, and the gates your auditors rely on stay exactly where they were.

Platform engineering

Scale the platform without scaling the queue.

Ingesting a Platform Library overnight

The problem

Your modules, pipelines and infrastructure code took years to build, and the knowledge of how they fit together lives in a few engineers' heads.

What seaLeye does

The discovery agent reads the repositories that hold your Platform Library, read-only, and learns the control model that governs them. Each module it understands becomes available to the front door agent.

What changes

Overnight, the front door knows your platform, and you get a written map of what your developers could serve themselves today.

Knowing exactly what your platform is missing

The problem

When a developer needs something the platform does not offer, they either wait with no answer or build it themselves outside your controls. Either way, you never hear about it.

What seaLeye does

seaLeye drafts the plan, records the need with the code that shows it, and routes it to the platform team. sealeye needs is the platform team's ledger: every need, the apps waiting on it, and a request ready to paste into a ticket.

What changes

Nothing gets built around the library, and the roadmap is written from evidence. Each need is requested until the platform team agrees to it, planned once they have, and available once the module ships.

Business-line leaders

Weeks to minutes, on the platform you already paid for.

Managing what is running, in seaLeye.online

The problem

Environments outlive the projects that asked for them. Nobody can say what each one costs, who owns it, or whether anyone still uses it.

What seaLeye does

seaLeye.online shows every environment seaLeye built, with its cost, its owner and what it is doing. Right-size it, put it to sleep when nobody is using it, or decommission it, from the console or the command line.

What changes

Spend follows use, every environment has an owner, and idle ones stop costing money.

Putting the freed capacity to work

The problem

Your best platform engineers spend their days provisioning for other teams, and the strategic work has been waiting for years.

What seaLeye does

Developers serve themselves, so the everyday requests leave the queue and forward-deployed engineers come home. Plans describe what an app needs, not how it is built, so a change in the library reaches every app.

What changes

The platform team's time goes to multi-cloud, to paying down tech debt in pipelines and infrastructure code, and to adding the services AI applications need.

Risk and audit

The same controls, with better evidence.

Answering the auditor from the record

The problem

An auditor asks who requested an environment, what was approved, what was built and whether it is still that. The answer is assembled by hand from tickets, logs and memory.

What seaLeye does

sealeye evidence writes the change record for one environment: who asked, the plan they approved, what was built, and what the cloud says about it right now. Every value is read from the record or checked live. Nothing is typed in, and no secret's value ever appears.

What changes

The auditor gets a document generated from the record, and change records are ready for your ITSM.
