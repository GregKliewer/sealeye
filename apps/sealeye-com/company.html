Company

We ran the platform team. The door was the problem.

seaLeye was founded by two people who spent years building a bank's developer platform, and then found that the hard part was getting developers through its door.

The product was hiding inside an adoption problem

Dana Whitlock and Sam Okafor ran the developer platform group at Atlantic Seaboard Bancorp, one of the biggest banks on the East Coast. They had the pipelines, the templates and the golden paths a bank's platform is supposed to have, and almost nobody used them unless they were made to.

They tried better documentation, roadshows and a portal. Adoption moved when a mandate moved it and stopped when the mandate did. Eventually they admitted what had gone wrong: they had been asking developers to come to the platform. Developers live in a terminal, in a folder, with an app that is not running yet. So they went there instead.

Sam wrote a command over a weekend. From inside your folder, it built you a dev environment and put your code in it. No form, no ticket, no reading. It was meant to be a wedge. Within a quarter, teams across the bank had run it, and then they came back asking for the rest: a change to what it built, a look at what was running, a way to move a build through the test stages, a service nobody at the bank had used before.

That flood of requests was the product. When agentic build became real, the two partners brought it out of the bank for every platform team with the same problem. Today, seaLeye is deployed at some of the largest financial institutions in North America.

Principles

What the product already lives by

Through the platform, not around it

seaLeye builds only from approved modules, through your pipelines, under your policies. The controls you certified are the controls that run.

Evidence, not assertion

Status mirrors what the cloud says, and every change record is generated from the record. Nothing is typed in, and a value that could not be read says so.

Say no in writing

When an app needs something the library does not offer, seaLeye records the need with the code that shows it, rather than improvising or pretending.

One product, two surfaces

The command line and the console do exactly the same things. Developers choose the interface; the platform stays the same.
